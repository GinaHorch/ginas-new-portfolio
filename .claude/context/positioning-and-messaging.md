### ASD Essentials (Australian context)

The Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) released the ASD Essentials series via the ACSC in October 2026 as the evolution of the Essential Eight. It is the current Australian reference point for enterprise security posture, so a Perth-based analyst who understands it signals up-to-date GRC awareness. Source: cyber.gov.au (see "Sources and verification" below).

Facts to use accurately:

What it is: a principles-based, outcome-focused framework. The principles are drawn from the cyber security principles in ASD's Information Security Manual (ISM) and align with ASD's Modern Defensible Architecture.
Series structure: separate chapters per technology environment, starting with Enterprise IT, Operational Technology (OT) and Cloud. Each chapter uses the same three layers: principles (the security outcomes), actions (what is done to achieve them) and domains (groupings of business functions and assets that share similar risk).
Enterprise IT chapter: six technical domains grouped into priority phases (a sequencing mechanism, not a maturity model), plus two capability domains that apply across all phases: Governance and culture and Visibility and response.
Assessment: organisations rate the effectiveness of actions per domain, considering coverage, consistency, currency and evidence quality, supported by "Indicators of Good Practice" and an assessment evidence guide. The assessment process includes a MITRE ATT&CK mapping. It replaces the Essential Eight maturity levels (ML1 to ML3), and old maturity ratings do not convert automatically into the new ratings.
What is new beyond the Essential Eight: principles such as content filtering, cyber security awareness training and cyber supply chain security, plus a dedicated Visibility and response domain covering event logging, detection, incident identification, detection efficacy and incident coordination, and a Govern area that includes executive accountability (including for AI).
Transition: ASD has said the Essential Eight will be phased out over roughly two years, and both frameworks are being maintained during the transition, with transitional arrangements continuing into 2027. Every Essential Eight strategy maps to at least one Essentials principle, and ASD publishes a transition guide.

Why it fits this positioning:

Visibility and response is the SOC domain: logging, detection, incident coordination. It is the natural bridge between Gina's SIEM/IR training and GRC.
Governance and culture matches the risk-communication and documentation strengths from investigations and leadership briefings.
Her Entra ID, Intune, Defender, MFA, patching, hardening and encryption work lines up with the identity, endpoint and vulnerability-related actions, which makes the live-environment work easier to explain in a framework hiring managers recognise.
Thinking in terms of "does the action achieve its security outcome, with evidence?" is the same discipline as writing up an investigation so findings stand up.

Level and wording rules (important):

Present this as working knowledge / currently developing unless Gina has actually applied or assessed against it. Do not claim to have implemented, assessed or been audited against the Essentials, and do not say she "migrated" an organisation from the Essential Eight.
Do not claim Essential Eight maturity ratings for the SMSF business unless Gina personally did that work and can say so accurately.
Suggested wording: "I follow ASD's guidance closely, including the new ASD Essentials series that is replacing the Essential Eight, and I map my identity, endpoint and incident-response work to its principles." Only use "map" if she has done a mapping. Otherwise: "I'm studying the new ASD Essentials series, which is evolving the Essential Eight."
A good portfolio item: a short, honest write-up mapping her own sanitised identity/endpoint controls (or a lab environment) to the Enterprise IT principles and domains, stating clearly which actions are evidenced and which are gaps.
Do not copy text from ASD publications. Summarise in her own words and link to the source.

Framework references (for example ASD Essentials and the ISM, the Essential Eight during the transition, ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF, MITRE ATT&CK for detection mapping) may be named **only as to the level actually studied or applied**:

- studied or referenced in work → "working knowledge" or "familiar with"
- applied to a real assessment → say what was done
- being studied now → "currently developing"

Do not claim formal GRC certification, audit experience or compliance ownership that Gina has not held. If a regulated-environment responsibility (for example regulatory reporting) was not hers, do not imply it.

Suggested framing: "I treat GRC as the foundation of security work and I'm actively building depth in it."

## SOC and incident-response direction

SOC capability should be shown through **write-ups**, not claims.

- Publish three to five sanitised, honest, lab-labelled write-ups using this structure: Summary → Objective → Environment and tools → Approach → Findings → Assessment and decision → MITRE ATT&CK mapping → What I learned / would improve.
- Aim for range: one alert triage (phishing, brute force or suspicious login), one SIEM query or dashboard project (Splunk or Elastic), one endpoint/EDR or suspicious-process investigation, one MITRE ATT&CK mapping of an incident, and one OSINT or threat-research task.
- Explain reasoning ("I checked X first because…"), not only results. A screenshot-only write-up is not evidence.
- Decisions matter: what would be escalated, contained or closed, and why. This is where the detective background becomes visible.
- Sanitise everything: names, tenant IDs, credentials, API keys, company data. Never publish live malware. Describe what was analysed.
- Never publish anything from the SMSF employer or any other employer that is not publicly cleared. Describe the environment generically.

Introductory offensive-security lab work (SQL injection, reconnaissance, OSINT, vulnerability scanning against Metasploitable) is described as **blue-team-relevant fundamentals, clearly marked as controlled lab work**.
